What image viewers do
that makes people leave.

A survey of the complaints that irritated more than one person across seven popular Windows image viewers — ranked by discussion volume: how much was posted and how many distinct people chimed in. Great software removes friction more than it adds features; this is the empirical map of where the friction lives, so a new viewer can pre-avoid it.

Where the friction clusters

The same patterns recur across unrelated viewers. Counted by how many of the seven viewers produced multi-person complaints of each kind.

Zoom/navigation behavior and fullscreen/slideshow quirks dominate, followed by startup speed and “the viewer lies” problems — wrong resampling, wrong colors, embedded previews standing in for real data. That ordering is the design brief: input behavior and pixel honesty hurt more people than missing features do.

The pre-avoidance list

Each finding above converts into a rule a new viewer can hold itself to. These are the design commitments the evidence supports — what “not hurting the user” looks like in practice.

What this survey couldn't settle

  • XnView's own forum couldn't be read. newsgroup.xnview.com was blocked in the research environment, so XnView's ranking rests on changelogs, reviews, and one multi-user thread — it's the least certain placement on the list.
  • nomacs volumes weren't verified one by one. The nomacs cluster is ranked on issue count plus a secondary research document that cites the issues; individual comment counts may vary.
  • qView's evidence is fork-shaped. Its tracker is small, so its irritations show up as two maintained forks and changelog entries rather than high-comment threads — real pain, measured differently.
  • IrfanView forum counts are approximate. Thread URLs carry trailing slugs and fetched text showed subsets of posts, so reply counts are estimates, not exact tallies.

Sources are linked under each finding. Two sources were read live on the research date (marked “read live”); the rest come from search-index content. Reply counts and poster counts are approximations from what the pages showed.

Survey conducted October 2, 2026, across the IrfanView forum, GitHub issue trackers, ElevenForum, Tom's Hardware, DPReview, Pentax Forums, ResetEra, and review sites.
